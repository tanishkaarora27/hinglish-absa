No public dataset of **quick-commerce (Blinkit / Zepto / Instamart) Hinglish reviews with aspect + sarcasm labels** exists as far as I could find — that is exactly the gap, and why the project collects and labels its own data.

## 3. Collecting real quick-commerce reviews — method and ethics

**Tool:** `google-play-scraper` (PyPI, MIT licence, pure Python, no API key). `reviews(app_id, lang, country, sort, count, continuation_token)` returns review text, star score, date, thumbs-up count and a continuation token for paging (confirmed from the PyPI documentation). Package IDs confirmed from app-store index pages: Blinkit `com.grofers.customerapp`, Zepto `com.zeptoconsumerapp`; Swiggy Instamart lives inside the Swiggy app `in.swiggy.android` (verify the `?id=` in the Play Store URL before running).

**Why it is legal/ethical enough for an academic project:** reviews are public, no login or access-control bypass, we keep only text + rating + date + app (user names are dropped before saving), we throttle requests and collect a small, non-commercial research sample. State this on a slide and in the report; also say that Google Play's Terms of Service govern the site and that the data is not redistributed.

**Measured yield:** `lang="en", country="in"` returns mostly English + Romanised Hinglish. In our run the code-mixed share was lower than expected: at a ≥15% Hindi-token threshold only ~16% of usable reviews qualified (584 of 3,688 after de-duplication), so we scraped deeper (three sort orders, up to 10,000 per app) and relaxed the LID threshold to ≥10% Hindi tokens, which gave ~8,550 code-mixed reviews; 2,500 were sampled for labelling, balanced across star ratings. Report your own ratio — it depends on when and how deep you scrape.

**Labelling plan:** 1,500–3,000 reviews, 7 aspect columns (`pos/neg/neu` or blank) + `sarcastic` (0/1); ideally two annotators on ≥200 shared reviews → Cohen's kappa (`prepare_labelled_data.py --sheet2`). Our 2,500 reviews were labelled in a single pass with a second targeted pass for sarcasm. Full rules with worked examples: `docs/01_labelling_guidelines.md`. The sheet is pre-filled with the rule-based extractor's guesses (marked `?`) to speed up annotation; the annotator must confirm or correct each.

## 4. What this means for the design (one paragraph)
Published work either stops at sentence-level sentiment (SentiMix), uses synthetic Devanagari ABSA data (ABSA-Mix), or detects sarcasm on tweets with GPU models (Shah & Maurya; Mazumder et al.). None combines **real Roman-script reviews + aspect-level sentiment + explicit sarcasm correction** in a CPU-only, explainable system. That combination is the project's contribution; the public datasets above give us (a) a trained LID/CMI module and (b) an honest, real benchmark for the sarcasm component, while the quick-commerce dataset you label is the project's main data.
